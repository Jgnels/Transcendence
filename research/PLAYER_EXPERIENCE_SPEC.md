# Player Experience Specification

## Stable owner preferences

- Approximately 2,000+ hours across Total War games going back to Medieval II.
- Exclusively single-player except for Three Kingdoms campaigns played with the owner's brother.
- Roughly half of WH3 campaigns are played as Karl Franz.
- Strong thematic preference for ordinary humans prevailing against superior supernatural beings and monsters.
- Enjoys winning battles against materially unfavorable odds.
- Usually plays the hardest campaign and battle difficulties.
- Often uses ironman mode.
- Definitely uses the owner's described “battlefield limitation mode”; exact in-game setting name and configuration must be frozen from the installation.
- SFO: Grimhammer III is the favorite and intended primary baseline.
- Late-game challenge collapse is the largest current problem.
- Three Kingdoms is the strongest official-series reference for late-game and empire-management feel.

## Desired experience

1. Early game remains tense without requiring a scripted anti-player dogpile.
2. Midgame rewards good strategic play but creates coherent regional opponents.
3. Late game forms rival blocs, existential threats, coordinated invasions, strategic dilemmas and meaningful costs of empire.
4. Battles remain winnable through exceptional command even when the strategic odds are poor.
5. Losing a battle or settlement can produce a compelling recovery campaign rather than an inevitable spiral or meaningless inconvenience.
6. Factions retain thematic doctrine without becoming predictable caricatures.
7. SFO's slower, lore-oriented and higher-impact battle/campaign feel is preserved unless a measured change improves it.

## Anti-goals

- Constant anti-player targeting.
- Pure stat inflation presented as smarter AI.
- Endless disposable army spam.
- Every faction coordinating magically against the player.
- Removing the possibility of heroic battlefield victories.
- Punishing expansion solely with arbitrary debuffs.
- Making the optimal response tedious rather than difficult.
- Optimizing win rate while reducing campaign variety or narrative coherence.

## Reference design concepts from Three Kingdoms

Treat these as design inspiration to test, not mechanics to copy blindly:

- rival claimants and late-game power centers;
- coalitions, alliances, vassals and negotiated statecraft;
- diplomacy as a first-class strategic system;
- escalation tied to rank and power;
- empire size creating new strategic obligations and enemies;
- strong late-game factions remaining politically relevant.
