WH3 8.1.x possesses a deep, table-driven Campaign AI architecture that has evolved continuously since Rome II. It is not a simple scripted opponent. Native systems include: MCTS-based task allocation; dual threat/strength evaluation scales; query-driven and influence-map-driven threat detection; turn-distance-scaled task priority with dynamic horizon expansion; faction-potential-based autoresolve and shrouded-AI bonuses; personality-driven budget pools; pooled-resource recruitment budgeting; and turn-phase-dependent priority modulation. Most of these systems are opaque at runtime but configurable via database tables and observable via scripting interfaces.
An external AI project that rebuilds any of these without first proving native failure risks massive redundancy.

FORCE YOURSELF TO BE WRONG
I must assume my own analysis contains fatal errors. Here are the most likely ways this atlas misleads:
Table-name hallucination — I have inferred table names like cai_influence_map and cai_task_generator from CA blog terminology, but I have not verified these exact strings in RPFM schemas. They may be named entirely differently (e.g., cai_influence_maps, cai_task_types, or be hardcoded with no table exposure). Any mod design assuming these table names without schema verification will crash.
MCTS overstatement — The GameDeveloper article and CA blog describe MCTS as central to Rome II's task allocation. WH3 may have replaced or heavily modified this core since 2013. I am treating Rome II architecture as WH3 heritage without proof that the same MCTS kernel survives in 8.1.x. It may now be heuristic-driven or hybrid.
Query/Influence map duality oversimplification — CA describes two mechanisms, but there may be 3–4 additional threat-detection layers (e.g., diplomatic event buffers, region-ownership delta tracking, scripted crisis injectors) that I have collapsed into "query system."
Threat/Strength conflation — I present threat and strength as cleanly separated scales. In practice, they may interpenetrate in ways CA has not disclosed (e.g., strength evaluations feeding back into threat scores after battles).
Patch-note survivorship bias — CA only documents fixes that shipped. I have no visibility into fixes that were attempted and reverted, or into systems that were deprecated (e.g., early WH3 "Realms of Chaos" narrative AI may have left dead code in the CAI pipeline).
Modding interface completeness fallacy — The cm: scripting interface documentation I cited is community-maintained and may lag behind engine reality. Functions marked as available may be partially broken in 8.1.x, and new interfaces may exist undocumented.
Anti-player bias narrative — CA's Beta 2 blog frames anti-player bias as primarily threat-system-driven. It could equally be driven by hardcoded campaign scenario scripts (e.g., end-game crises targeting player by design) that are untouched by the threat multiplier changes.
Pooled-resource fix overconfidence — 7.0 and 6.3.2 fixed known pooled-resource failures. With ~200 resources and DLC adding new economic mechanics every quarter, the budgeting system is likely still brittle for edge cases (e.g., multi-step crafting resources like Chaos Dwarf manufactories).
Distance-scaling scope error — I cite dynamic distance scaling as a solved problem in 8.1, but CA's description is vague about whether this applies globally or only to specific personality archetypes. It may not affect horde factions, sea factions, or teleport-capable factions.
Battle AI scripting accessibility — I cite community claims that campaign battles can be scripted. This may require specific battle types or may have been broken by 8.1 engine changes. Treating BAI as "influenceable" without runtime proof is dangerous.
