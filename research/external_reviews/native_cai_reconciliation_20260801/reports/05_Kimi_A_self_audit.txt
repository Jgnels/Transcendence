8. NATIVE FORCE ALLOCATION CLAIM
Proven for current WH3:
Target strength evaluation before force selection. 
Existing owned armies evaluated for strength ratio against target. 
Distance (turns) considered. 
Stance considered (tunneling, forced march). 
Recruitment considered if no suitable force exists. 
Not proven:
Exclusivity (one army per task)
Temporal commitment (army locked to task)
Anti-thrashing (rapid reassignment prevention)
Reserve protection (core garrison preservation)
Recovery protection (wounded army resting)
Conclusion: WH3 demonstrably possesses more information than a pure geometric allocator (it evaluates strength ratios, stances, and recruitment time), but the full assignment semantics are opaque.

12. ONE FINAL QUESTION
Three Most Sophisticated Current-WH3 Campaign AI Capabilities (Definitely Proven)
Dual-scale threat/strength evaluation with personality- and difficulty-modulated multipliers. The CAI maintains separate threat and strength scales, applies asymmetric multipliers to player factions (historically 50–200%, revised toward 90–120%), and uses these scores to drive diplomacy, task generation, and deal evaluation. 
Turn-distance-scaled task priority with stance-aware pathing and dynamic horizon expansion. The CAI measures task distance in turns, accounts for movement extents and stances (tunneling, forced march), adjusts priority floors based on faction size (15+/30+ region thresholds), and in 8.1 further modulates by campaign turn count.  
Pooled-resource-aware recruitment budgeting with unit-cap validation. The CAI constructs recruitment shopping lists using beliefs about cheapest, strongest, and cost-efficient units; post-6.3.2 it respects unit caps; post-7.0 it budgets pooled resources (Blessed Spawnings, Oathgold, etc.) through dedicated personality allocation junctions and decision-policy budget columns.  
Three Things Outsiders Commonly Assume WH3 CAI Does That I Cannot Prove
MCTS-based task allocation. Many assume the Rome II/Attila MCTS architecture survives intact in WH3. No WH3 dev blog, patch note, or technical article confirms MCTS is the current algorithm. The task allocation system is real and complex; its algorithmic core is unverified.
Database-table-configurable turn-dependent priority modulation. The 8.1 mechanism is proven to exist, but CA provided no table name, schema entry, or script interface. Outsiders often assume every new CAI feature is automatically exposed in cai_* tables. This one is not proven to be.
Real-time scripted BAI control in normal campaign battles. The battle script API (AIObjectivePlanner, unit controllers) is robust for scripted battles (historical, quest). Outsiders commonly extrapolate this to mean modders can seize real-time control of BAI during normal campaign battles. Normal campaign battle scripting is limited to pre-battle setup (add_custom_battlefield) and post-battle effects; real-time BAI override during standard campaign fights is unverified.
