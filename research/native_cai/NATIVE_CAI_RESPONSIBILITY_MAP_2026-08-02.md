## Architecture verdict after real row evidence

The native-primary decision is **strengthened, not falsified**.

The decisive new fact is not merely that native CAI “has an allocator.” Its current data contains allocator-specific variables for distance scoring, recruitment, force release and force return, while current task-generator data contains explicit time-dependent/endgame priority configuration. Experienced mods demonstrably alter these native controls.

Therefore a project-owned strategic allocator would have to outperform an engine-owned, context-rich allocator **after** controlled tuning of its existing exposed knobs. Explainability alone is insufficient justification to replace it.

## What remains genuinely open

The high-value unknowns are now narrower:

1. Does native CAI misuse damaged/recovering armies at a measurable rate?
2. Does it deliberately maintain adequate reserves?
3. Does it double-book or rapidly reverse army intent?
4. How persistent are native assignments across turns?
5. Can small DB changes fix the observable late-game idling / allocation failures without second-order regressions?

These are behavioral ablation questions, not reasons to resume v0.2I assignment-feasibility transport.
